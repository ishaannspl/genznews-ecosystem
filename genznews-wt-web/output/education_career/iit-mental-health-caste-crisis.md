## The Hook

The rising suicide rates among IIT students highlight a crisis that demands our attention. It's not just about grades; it's about the silent struggles behind the numbers.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- Suicides among IIT students reflect deeper societal issues, especially related to caste.
- Many students from marginalized backgrounds face overwhelming pressure and limited support.
- Empathy and inclusivity in academia are crucial for addressing these mental health challenges.

## The Breakdown

The tragic increase in student suicides at IITs underscores a critical need for change in how we view academic pressure. It’s not just about merit but also about the societal factors, like caste, that contribute to these pressures.

Students, often first-generation learners from marginalized communities, face immense expectations with little support. This disconnect between academic rigor and mental health resources leads to devastating outcomes, including dropout rates and suicides.

## Why It Matters

For young people in high-pressure environments, recognizing the impact of social issues like caste is vital. Advocating for mental health resources and fostering inclusive spaces can help combat the stigma and create supportive educational environments.
