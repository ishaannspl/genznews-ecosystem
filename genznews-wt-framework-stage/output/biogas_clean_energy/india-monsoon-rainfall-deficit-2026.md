## The Hook

India just wrapped up its southwest monsoon with a 13 percent deficit, making it the fourth-lowest rainfall season recorded since 2001. If erratic weather keeps threatening traditional agriculture, energy systems must adapt fast.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- India received 759.4 mm of rainfall against the normal 868.6 mm during the June to September season.
- The IMD blames the El Nino weather pattern for driving the weaker precipitation and uneven regional distribution.
- Despite late-season storms narrowing the gap, regions like South India and Punjab faced massive rainfall shortages.

## The Breakdown

The India Meteorological Department reported that the monsoon finished 12.6 percent below average. June kicked off with a massive 35 percent deficit, the highest in 12 years, after the seasonal rains stalled for nearly two weeks.

While late-season storms formed over the Bay of Bengal to rescue some regions from total drought, the overall impact was severely uneven. Odisha celebrated its wettest monsoon in 20 years, while East and Northeast India faced historic dryness.

## Why It Matters

Unpredictable monsoons hit food security and power grids hard, making decentralized clean energy sources like biogas and solar crucial for rural resilience. When traditional rainfall fails, rural communities need reliable, locally produced energy alternatives to keep farms and homes running without relying on strained fossil fuels.
