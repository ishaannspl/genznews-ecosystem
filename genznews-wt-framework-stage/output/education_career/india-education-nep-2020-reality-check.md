## The Hook

Scoring 99 percent does not guarantee you can solve actual problems. Six years after the National Education Policy 2020 promised to end rote learning, the gap between reform on paper and classroom reality is wider than ever.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- Six years into NEP 2020, teacher shortages and intense coaching cultures still dominate Indian schooling.
- Rote testing fuels severe teenage anxiety while leaving graduates unprepared for modern workplace demands.
- Meaningful reform requires replacing high-stakes final exams with continuous low-stakes assessments and practical projects.

## The Breakdown

Every admissions cycle plays out the exact same way across India. Cut-offs climb to absurd highs, coaching centers plaster billboards with top rankers, and students who miss out face intense scrutiny. Despite national policies designed to build critical thinking and vocational training, rural schools lack basic resources while urban hubs remain trapped in test-prep mania.

The fallout goes far beyond report cards. Academic pressure remains a leading cause of teenage anxiety and mental health crises nationwide. At the same time, employers increasingly report that candidates who ace memorisation tests still struggle with problem-solving, collaboration, and basic workplace adaptability.

## Why It Matters

Your real-world value will not be determined by how cleanly you regurgitate a syllabus under exam hall lights. Until schools and hiring pipelines reward actual execution over raw test percentages, young people will keep burning out before their careers even take off.
