## The Hook

Think twice before swapping spit. Health advocates warn that a simple kiss remains the primary gateway for transmitting lifelong herpes infections.

## TL;DR (Quick Hits)

- The Australian Herpes Management Forum warned that kissing is a primary transmission route for HSV-1 and HSV-2.
- Once contracted, the virus stays in your system permanently and can spread even when visible sores are absent.
- Health officials launched an awareness campaign to stop adults from accidentally passing cold sores to kids and partners.

## The Breakdown

The Australian Herpes Management Forum launched a national campaign spotlighting a blunt reality: casual kisses easily spread the Herpes Simplex Virus. The health warning applies across the board, from couples hooking up to well-meaning relatives pecking young kids on the cheek.

Executive Director Tricia Berger emphasized that HSV-1, commonly known as cold sores, stays in your body permanently after initial contact. While transmission risk peaks during active outbreaks, the virus can also spread silently when no visible sores are present.

## Why It Matters

Stigma around cold sores is high, but understanding how common transmission works keeps you and your circle protected. Normalizing health boundaries and skipping physical contact during active flare-ups is just smart dating etiquette.
